# DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

BIOSPHERE is built on a small set of convictions, held firmly enough to say no with.

## Reflection over productivity

Most habit software optimizes throughput: check faster, streak longer, do more. BIOSPHERE optimizes honesty. The day's only accounting happens once, at night, in words as well as booleans, because the sentence "I sat down but couldn't focus" is worth more to a person than a broken streak counter. Metrics exist (Stillness) but they whisper.

## Presence over efficiency

We deliberately spend seconds where a dashboard would spend milliseconds: a 1.8-second scene crossfade, a one-second threshold into immersion, an interface that takes 1.6 seconds to dissolve. Slowness here is not waste — it is the medium. The user is being taught, gently, that this is a place and not a queue.

## Beauty, calm, silence, and minimalism are functional

A scene that is genuinely beautiful gets opened again tomorrow; that is retention engineering by other means. Calm lowers the cost of honesty at night. Silence between birdsong makes the birdsong real. Every control removed is attention returned to the world. None of these are decoration; they are the mechanism.

## No dopamine mechanics — ever

No confetti, badges, levels, flames, red counters, variable rewards, or celebratory interruptions. The reward for fulfilling a habit is a filled grey dot and the private knowledge of having done it. This is a permanent refusal, not a v1 omission (see BACKLOG.md).

## The interface disappears behind the experience

Chrome exists to be forgotten. Inside a biosphere the UI literally fades to a ghost. On the home screen the world shows through every panel. Menus are confined to one Settings page; the rest of the app has almost nothing to operate.

## Restraint is craft

Never add a feature because other apps have it. Never sacrifice immersion for convenience, or simplicity for functionality. Every feature must justify its existence by improving immersion — VISION.md's test is the gate: *does this make the world more real, or the app more present?*

## Anti-patterns we refuse

Unnecessary notifications and menus. Buttons that exist "just in case." Daytime checkboxes. Nagging copy ("You haven't…"). Guilt as a retention strategy. Engagement loops of any kind. If BIOSPHERE is ever addictive, we have failed at something more important than growth.
